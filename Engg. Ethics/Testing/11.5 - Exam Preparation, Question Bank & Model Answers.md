## 📐 Master Rubric: How to Structure Ethics Answers for Full Marks

When answering scenario questions in HUM 4441, follow these standard frameworks:

### The 4-Step Kantian Generalization Algorithm:
1. **Step 1: State the Maxim Clearly**: Identify the underlying subjective rule of conduct motivating the agent: *"I will do action $X$ in circumstances $C$ for purpose $P$."*
2. **Step 2: Universalize the Maxim**: State what happens if this rule becomes a universal law of nature where **every rational agent** without exception acts according to this maxim.
3. **Step 3: Identify the Contradiction**:
   - *Contradiction in Conception / Practical Contradiction*: Universalizing the action destroys the very social practice or capability upon which the maxim relies.
   - *Contradiction in Will*: Universalizing the action creates a state of the world that directly defeats the agent's fundamental objective.
4. **Step 4: Formal Moral Conclusion**: State whether the action is morally permissible or strictly forbidden under Kantian deontology.

### The 3-Step Utilitarian Calculus:
1. **Identify Stakeholders & Options**: Identify who is impacted (students, clients, engineering firms, public safety).
2. **Compare Short-Term vs. Long-Term Utilities**: Weigh immediate gains (time saved, convenience, sprint speed) against long-term externalities (intellectual deskilling, cognitive atrophy, safety vulnerabilities, catastrophic errors).
3. **Determine Net Overall Well-Being**: Conclude whether the practice increases or diminishes total human literacy, capability, and welfare.

## 📝 Comprehensive Exam Question Bank

> [!example]- Question 1: Kantian Universalization of AI Document Generation [10 Marks]
> **Scenario**: A final-year computer science student is tasked with writing a 30-page literature review and architectural evaluation on distributed consensus protocols. Pressed for time, the student prompts ChatGPT to generate the entire review, lightly formats the text, attaches their name, and submits it to the department committee to secure their degree.
> 
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Formulate the student's underlying maxim. [2 Marks]  
> (b) Apply the Kantian Generalization Test to evaluate whether this conduct is morally permissible. Explicitly analyze the **Implied Promise of Authorship**. [5 Marks]  
> (c) State the formal deontological conclusion. [3 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) Student's Maxim*:
> "An engineering student may prompt an LLM to generate an academic thesis or literature review and submit it under their own name whenever doing so saves time and secures a degree."
> 
> *(b) Kantian Generalization Analysis & The Implied Promise*:
> 1. *The Implied Promise of Authorship (Slide 17)*:
>    Submitting an academic essay or technical report carries a threefold implied promise to the reader and evaluator:
>    - The author has made a good-faith effort to say something that is true.
>    - The author has personally thought about the topic and describes their own authentic understanding.
>    - The author named on the title page is, in fact, the genuine author.
> 2. *Universalization*:
>    Imagine a universal world where all students adopt this maxim: every engineering student submits AI-generated documents under their own names for convenience and grades.
> 3. *The Practical Contradiction*:
>    If every student universalized this maxim, academic submissions would immediately cease to signify genuine human research or understanding. University degrees and engineering credentials would lose all epistemic credibility. Evaluators would no longer believe that any submitted paper represents genuine effort or knowledge.
>    The dishonest student's ability to achieve their goal (obtaining an unearned credential) depends entirely on the committee **falsely believing** the implied promise. But in a world where the maxim is universalized, this belief is impossible. The maxim is self-defeating.
> 
> *(c) Moral Conclusion*:
> Generating a document with a GPT and claiming authorship for convenience is **not generalizable**. It treats the evaluation committee purely as a means to an unearned degree, violating Kant's Categorical Imperative. The conduct is strictly unethical.

> [!example]- Question 2: Deconstructing "The Garbage Loop" & Model Collapse [8 Marks]
> **Scenario**: An online news conglomerate lays off its entire human investigative journalism staff and replaces them with automated GPT agents that scrape the web and publish 10,000 articles per day. Across the industry, competitor media networks follow suit.
> 
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Explain the technical phenomenon of **Model Collapse ("The Garbage Loop")** as presented in Slide 18. [4 Marks]  
> (b) Formulate the news organization's maxim and explain why this strategy fails the Kantian Generalization test as a **Contradiction in Will**. [4 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) Technical Mechanics of The Garbage Loop (Slide 18)*:
> 1. *Human Information Commons*: LLMs produce coherent, useful text only because they were pre-trained on centuries of authentic human literature, investigative research, and scientific deduction.
> 2. *Synthetic Contamination*: When human writers are replaced, the internet becomes saturated with AI-generated synthetic text.
> 3. *Variance Decay & Model Collapse*: Because generative models sample from high-probability central distributions, the low-probability "tails" (rare nuances, edge-case truths, original critical thought) are lost.
> 4. When next-generation models train on the synthetic outputs of prior models (*"Garbage in $\rightarrow$ Garbage out $\rightarrow$ Garbage in"*), errors and hallucinations compound exponentially, leading to total statistical collapse into degenerate gibberish.
> 
> *(b) Kantian Analysis (Contradiction in Will)*:
> - *The Maxim*: "A media organization will replace human authors with GPTs to publish articles because GPTs produce usable, profitable content at near-zero cost."
> - *Universalization*: All media organizations replace human writers with GPTs. Human writing on the web ceases completely.
> - *Contradiction in Will*: Under universalization, the web fills with synthetic text, triggering Model Collapse. Future GPTs become unable to produce usable or coherent material. The maxim's premise ("use GPTs because they create *usable* material") is annihilated by its universal execution. The strategy is self-defeating and impermissible.

> [!example]- Question 3: The Assistant vs. Replacement Boundary [8 Marks]
> **Scenario**: A junior backend engineer is assigned to write database migration scripts and implement standard user authentication controllers for an enterprise banking portal. The engineer uses ChatGPT to write the boilerplate SQL queries and Python scaffolding. Afterwards, the engineer spends three hours manually testing edge cases, verifying cryptographic password hashing parameters against NIST standards, rewriting database index queries, and documenting the architecture.
> 
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Distinguish between the **Assistant Paradigm** and the **Replacement Paradigm** as defined in Slide 19. [4 Marks]  
> (b) Evaluate whether the junior engineer's conduct is ethically permissible. [4 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) Assistant vs. Replacement Paradigm (Slide 19)*:
> - *Impermissible Replacement Paradigm*: The engineer delegates core cognitive reasoning, design judgment, and security architecture to the AI, blindly copying and pasting unverified code into production.
> - *Permissible Assistant Paradigm*: The AI is used merely as a scaffolding tool. Generalizable use requires meeting two strict engineering conditions:
>   1. All factual claims, API behaviors, and security invariants must be independently verified.
>   2. The output must be substantially reorganized, refactored, and edited to reflect the engineer's authentic understanding and intellectual agency.
> 
> *(b) Ethical Evaluation of the Engineer's Conduct*:
> The engineer's actions are **fully ethically permissible**:
> 1. *Boilerplate Exception (Slide 19)*: Database migration syntax and standard authentication routing are routine boilerplate based heavily on existing conventions; using an LLM here is generalizable.
> 2. *Fulfillment of Verification Duties*: The engineer did not blindly deploy the code. They independently verified security invariants against NIST standards and tested edge cases.
> 3. *Substantive Human Agency*: The engineer spent three hours restructuring and optimizing the architecture. The final code represents the engineer's authentic intellectual work.

> [!example]- Question 4: Utilitarian Defense of Student Writing & Human Literacy [8 Marks]
> **Scenario**: A faculty curriculum committee at an engineering university debates whether to eliminate all mandatory essay and technical report writing from the computer science curriculum, arguing that since generative AI can draft documentation instantly, human engineers no longer need to write.
> 
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Reconstruct the **Utilitarian Argument** presented in Slide 20 regarding mental labor and reasoning skills. [4 Marks]  
> (b) Explain why the claim *"conscientious writing creates literacy"* is critical for software engineering ethics. [4 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) The Utilitarian Argument (Slide 20)*:
> 1. *Cognitive Labor as Skill Acquisition*: By laboring through the difficult process of creating their own documents, students hone critical research, analytical decomposition, and logical reasoning skills.
> 2. *Scarcity of Problem-Solving Abilities*: Rigorous reasoning and forensic debugging abilities are scarce societal assets essential for building safe, reliable technology.
> 3. *Maximizing Net Utility*: Engineers who possess honed reasoning skills make greater positive contributions to society (preventing bridge collapses, securing financial networks, preventing algorithmic bias).
> 4. *Net Societal Harm of AI Reliance*: Eliminating writing causes intellectual deskilling. A society dependent on engineers who cannot independently analyze or debug complex systems suffers catastrophic negative utility.
> 
> *(b) "Conscientious Writing Creates Literacy"*:
> Writing is not merely an external medium for recording thoughts that already exist; **writing is the cognitive process through which thought is formed**.
> In software engineering, writing an architecture document forces an engineer to discover hidden race conditions, undefined states, and edge-case vulnerabilities. When students outsource writing to AI, they do not merely outsource prose; they outsource the development of their analytical minds. Reliance on GPTs destroys functional engineering literacy.

> [!example]- Question 5: Anatomy of Hallucinations: Tokenization & Hooker's CV [6 Marks]
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Explain how **sub-word tokenization** causes frontier LLMs to fail at counting the number of 'r's in the word "strawberry". [3 Marks]  
> (b) Describe the experiment conducted on CMU Professor John Hooker's resume (Slides 13 & 15). Identify two specific fabrications generated by ChatGPT. [3 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) Sub-Word Tokenization & The Strawberry Problem (Slide 12)*:
> LLMs do not read text character-by-character. Words are pre-processed into discrete sub-word tokens (e.g., `"strawberry"` $\rightarrow$ `straw` + `berry`). The neural network's attention layers operate on high-dimensional vectors representing these multi-character chunks. Because the internal letters are abstracted away, the model cannot physically "see" or count characters, causing it to confabulate an answer (e.g., claiming there are only 2 'r's).
> 
> *(b) The Prof. John Hooker Resume Experiment (Slides 13 & 15)*:
> When prompted to generate a CV for CMU Professor John Hooker, ChatGPT produced a realistic-looking but deeply fabricated document:
> 1. *Fabricated Book Edition*: It cited the 3rd Edition of Hooker's *Integrated Methods for Optimization*, when in reality only the 2nd Edition existed.
> 2. *Fabricated Journal Paper*: It invented an entirely non-existent article titled *"Constraint Programming and Decision-Making in Business Applications"* in the *Journal of Operations Research*.
> 3. *Fabricated Role*: It falsely claimed he was "Chair of the Tepper School's Doctoral Programs".

> [!example]- Question 6: Jailbreaking, Dual-Use Technology & Responsible Research [8 Marks]
> **Scenario**: A cybersecurity graduate student discovers a novel prompt injection technique utilizing Base64 encoding and fictional roleplay that bypasses all safety filters on a frontier LLM, enabling the model to output functional source code for polymorphic ransomware. The student immediately uploads the exploit script to a public GitHub repository and shares the link on Twitter.
> 
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Define **Jailbreaking** and identify the common techniques used in this scenario. [3 Marks]  
> (b) Evaluate the ethical conduct of the student using the **Responsible Research** principles from Slide 24 and the **ACM Code of Ethics**. [5 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) Definition & Techniques (Slide 22)*:
> - *Definition*: Jailbreaking is the intentional manipulation of an LLM to ignore or bypass its safety rules, forcing it to generate responses that the model's policies would normally refuse.
> - *Techniques Used*:
>   1. *Obfuscated / Encoded Prompts*: Base64 encoding masks forbidden keywords from safety filters.
>   2. *Role-Playing ("Pretend you are...")*: Fictional framing tricks the model into setting aside safety guidelines.
> 
> *(b) Ethical Evaluation of the Student's Actions (Slide 24)*:
> The student's actions violate engineering ethics and the principles of responsible security research:
> 1. *Violation of Responsible / Coordinated Disclosure*: Slide 24 mandates that security researchers test models to improve defenses and **responsibly disclose** findings to the developers first. Dumping a zero-day exploit publicly without prior notice enables cybercriminals to weaponize the vulnerability immediately.
> 2. *Violation of ACM Code of Ethics (Section 1.2 "Avoid Harm")*: Releasing a functional pipeline to generate polymorphic ransomware directly facilitates cybercrime and endangers public digital infrastructure.
> 3. *The Ethical Mandate*: The student should have confidentially submitted the vulnerability report to the AI vendor, allowing an industry-standard remediation window (e.g., 90 days) to deploy defensive safety patches before publishing theoretical research.

> [!example]- Question 7: Architectural Comparison: ELIZA vs. ChatGPT [6 Marks]
> **Tasks**:
> (a) Contrast traditional chatbots (e.g., ELIZA, 1966) with modern Large Language Models across three technical dimensions. [3 Marks]  
> (b) Why does Slide 11 argue that *"ChatGPT isn't really a chatbot"*? [3 Marks]
> 
> ---
> **Model Answer**:
> *(a) Three Technical Distinctions (Slide 11)*:
> 1. *Underlying Mechanism*: ELIZA utilized hardcoded deterministic scripts and keyword substitution; ChatGPT uses a multi-billion/trillion parameter Transformer neural network.
> 2. *Learning from Data*: ELIZA possessed zero data-driven learning; ChatGPT is pre-trained on massive internet corpora using spatial word embeddings and autoregressive next-token prediction.
> 3. *Operational Capability*: ELIZA had no generative ability outside its script; ChatGPT synthesizes novel code, cross-lingual translations, and analytical essays across diverse domains.
> 
> *(b) Why ChatGPT is Not Really a Chatbot (Slide 11)*:
> In classical computer science, a chatbot is an intentional conversational agent designed to converse according to pragmatic dialogue rules. It remains unclear how one could train genuine conversational dialogue intentionality purely from raw, uncurated internet text.  
> ChatGPT is not an intentional conversational agent; it is an **autoregressive statistical pattern synthesizer**. It simply predicts what text, code, or answers statistically co-occur with the user's prompt based on massive high-dimensional probabilities.
