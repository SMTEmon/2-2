# 🎯 HUM 4441: Engineering Ethics - Complete Open Book Exam Cheat-Sheet

> [!TIP]
> **How to use this cheat-sheet:** This document covers all 7 chapters of the syllabus. Use the emojis and visual blocks to quickly scan for topics during the exam.

## 📖 1. Foundations & The Unknown (Chapter 1)
*   **Moral Autonomy:** The ability to think critically and independently about moral issues under pressure (cost, schedule, law). Professional ethics begins where passive obedience stops.
*   **Ethics vs. Law:** 
    *   Law sets the *minimum floor*. 
    *   What is legal is not always ethical (e.g., exposing people to unregulated toxins).
    *   Ethics matters most when the law is silent, late, or narrow.
*   **Managing the Unknown:** Uncertainty does not remove responsibility. Good practice means testing seriously, anticipating failure, and resisting fake certainty.

## 🏛️ 2. Professionalism & Codes of Ethics (Chapter 2)
### The 4 Key Attributes of a Profession
1.  **Sophisticated Skills, Judgment, & Discretion:** Non-routine work; high autonomy.
2.  **Extensive Formal Education:** University degree required.
3.  **Special Governing Societies:** Sets standards (e.g., IEEE, NSPE).
4.  **Significant Public Good:** Benefits society (e.g., public safety).

### Models of Professional Societies
*   **Social-Contract Model:** Exist to serve the public good in exchange for high status/pay (Engineering fits here).
*   **Business Model:** Exist as elite labor unions to protect member economics (Engineering does *not* fit here).

### Codes of Ethics (NSPE vs. IEEE)
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Canon 1 (Paramountcy Clause):** Public safety, health, and welfare ALWAYS take precedence over employer loyalty or business interests. If a boss orders an unsafe design, Canon 1 overrules loyalty.

## ⚖️ 3. Ethical Lenses & Global Traditions (Chapter 3)
### The Four Ethical Lenses
| Lens | Core Focus | Example Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Utilitarianism** | Maximize total societal benefit (Cost-Benefit). | *Does the overall benefit to society outweigh the public risk?* |
| **Duty Ethics** | Universal obligations (Honesty, avoid harm). | *You have a duty to warn the public, regardless of the cost.* |
| **Rights Ethics** | Protect fundamental human rights. | *Citizens have a right to informed consent about risks.* |
| **Virtue Ethics** | Character traits of the professional. | *An ethical engineer doesn't falsify data (dishonesty).* |

### Global Ethics Convergence
*   **Chinese (Confucian):** Virtues, interdependence of individual & community.
*   **Indian (Vedic):** Self-control, truth, restraint in service.
*   **Muslim (Qur'anic):** Humility, honesty, strong prohibition against false claims.
*   **Buddhist:** Avoiding destruction of life, sustainable development.

### Teamwork
*   **Vices:** Withholding info, missing work, "ballhogging" (robs others of the right to learn).
*   **Virtues:** Reliability, openness, shared responsibility.

## 🛠️ 4. Problem-Solving Techniques (Chapter 4)
*   **Issue Sorting:** Sort issues into **Factual** (what is known?), **Conceptual** (definitions, e.g., "Is this a bribe?"), and **Moral** (which principle applies?).
*   **Line-Drawing:** Use for gray areas. Define a **Negative Paradigm (NP)** (clearly bad) and **Positive Paradigm (PP)** (clearly good). Place the problem on the line to see which it is closer to.
*   **Flow Charting:** Use for sequential decisions. Good for identifying where a chain of errors leads to systemic failure.
*   **Resolving Conflicts:** Look for the **Creative Middle** (redesigning the situation so fewer values are sacrificed).

## 🚨 5. Risk, Safety, and Accidents (Chapter 5)
### The 6 Factors of Risk Perception (How people misjudge risk)
1.  **Voluntary vs. Involuntary:** People accept higher risks if they *choose* them (e.g., leaking info voluntarily) vs. if the risk is *imposed* on them.
2.  **Probability (Expected Risk):** People often misjudge the capabilities of detection or assume low probability equals no risk.
3.  **Cumulative vs. Isolated Effect:** People treat repeated actions as "small, isolated acts," ignoring that the **cumulative risk** over a long period becomes dangerously high.
4.  **Short-term vs. Long-term:** Immediate harm feels riskier than delayed harm.
5.  **Reversible vs. Irreversible:** Temporary damage vs. permanent consequences.
6.  **Threshold Levels:** Danger at high exposures vs. danger even at tiny exposures.

> [!NOTE]
> **Safety Design Hierarchy (From minimum to best):**
> 1. Legal Compliance $\rightarrow$ 2. Accepted Practice $\rightarrow$ 3. Explored Alternatives $\rightarrow$ **4. Designed for Foreseeable Misuse**. (A warning label does NOT replace a safe design).

### Types of Accidents
| Type | Definition / Cause | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Procedural** | Human error, ignoring rules, bad choices. | "Pilot error", rushing maintenance. |
| **Engineered** | Flaw in the design itself or materials. | Bridge breaking under normal stress. |
| **Systemic** | Complex chain of small failures across teams. | ValuJet Flight 592 (Handling $\rightarrow$ Labeling). |

## 💼 6. Rights, Responsibilities, & Whistle-Blowing (Chapter 6)
### Conflicts of Interest (COI)
> [!WARNING]
> **Be able to classify these:**
*   **🔴 Actual:** Compromises objective judgment *right now*. (e.g., Violating your NDA to actively leak).
*   **🟡 Potential:** Not a conflict yet, but easily could become one. (e.g., Being friends with a journalist looking for scoops).
*   **🔵 Apparent:** Looks like a conflict to outsiders, breeding distrust. (e.g., Traveling across the country to secretly meet a journalist).

### Confidentiality vs. Career Mobility
*   **The Dilemma:** Engineers have a right to career mobility, but carry *experiential knowledge* that cannot be "unlearned."
*   **The Rule:** The **burden of protecting confidentiality** falls entirely on the individual engineer.

### 🗣️ Whistle-Blowing Framework (The 4 Conditions)
To ethically blow the whistle (go external), **ALL FOUR** must be met:
1.  **Need:** Clear and important harm to avoid.
2.  **Proximity:** Firsthand knowledge/expertise (not just rumor).
3.  **Capability:** Reasonable chance of success.
4.  **Last Resort:** All internal channels have failed or been exhausted.
*(Note: You are OBLIGATED to blow the whistle ONLY if there is imminent, serious harm to life/health).*

## 🌱 7. Specific Ethical Issues (Chapter 7)
### Environmental Ethics
*   **Cost-Oblivious:** Make environment as clean as possible (Rights/Duty Ethics).
*   **Cost-Benefit:** Balance pollution reduction against economic costs (Utilitarianism). *Must ask: "Who pays and who benefits?"* to avoid Environmental Racism.

### Computer Ethics
*   **Illusion of Competence:** Easy-to-use software can make engineers trust outputs outside their expertise. Software *never* substitutes for good judgment.
*   **Autonomous Computers:** When failure can become disastrous, meaningful human control/fail-safes are required (e.g., Therac-25 lacked hardware interlocks).

### Research Ethics
*   **Pathological Science:** Self-deception. Discarding data that doesn't fit, answering criticisms with *ad hoc* excuses.
*   **Honesty:** The central virtue in investigation, reporting, and proper attribution.

## 📚 8. Quick Reference: Major Case Studies
*(Use these as comparisons to strengthen your exam answers!)*

| Case | Core Ethical Failure | Key Takeaway / Lesson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Space Shuttle Challenger** | Ignored warning signs for schedule pressure. | "Put on your management hat." Never trade safety for schedules. |
| **Ford Pinto** | Cost-benefit analysis valued money over human life. | Known vulnerabilities make the company responsible for the active choice of risk. |
| **Paradyne Computers** | Fraud, false bidding, conflict of interest. | Lying on bids and using insider influence is illegal and unethical. |
| **ValuJet Flight 592** | Systemic failure across organizations. | Safety depends on the whole chain. Complex systems hide risk. |
| **Therac-25** | Software flaw, lack of hardware redundancy. | Never rely solely on software for life-critical safety. Need physical fail-safes. |
| **BART** | Ignored engineers' warnings; fired whistleblowers. | Engineers have a right to conscientious refusal & professional judgment. |
| **Denver Airport Concrete** | Falsifying data to pass inspections. | "Engineering judgment" cannot be used as an excuse to alter actual data and commit fraud. |
| **Bhopal Disaster** | Negligence, bypassed safety systems. | Maintenance cutbacks creating systemic failure. Unacceptable risk to the public. |
