# 3. Covariance & Correlation

> [!abstract] Overview & Key Concepts
> This note covers bivariate probability distributions, focusing on **Covariance** (definitions, operational formulas, independence theorems, and algebraic proofs), the **Variance of Sum Theorem**, solving continuous joint PDF problems, and **Pearson's Correlation Coefficient ($\rho$)**.

---

## 3.1 Covariance of Two Random Variables

### Conceptual Definition
Covariance measures the joint variability and linear dependence between two random variables $X$ and $Y$. 
- If $X$ and $Y$ tend to increase together, covariance is **positive**.
- If one variable tends to increase while the other decreases, covariance is **negative**.

### Standard Mathematical Definition
Let $X$ and $Y$ be random variables with means $\mu_X = E[X]$ and $\mu_Y = E[Y]$ respectively. The covariance between $X$ and $Y$ is defined as:

$$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E\left[ (X - \mu_X)(Y - \mu_Y) \right]$$

### Operational Computation Formula
In practice, covariance is computed using the equivalent operational formula:

$$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - \mu_X \mu_Y = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y]$$

> [!note]- View Proof: Derivation of Operational Formula
> Expand the product inside the expectation:
> 
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E\left[ (X - \mu_X)(Y - \mu_Y) \right] = E\left[ XY - X\mu_Y - Y\mu_X + \mu_X \mu_Y \right]$$
> 
> By linearity of expectation:
> 
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - \mu_Y E[X] - \mu_X E[Y] + \mu_X \mu_Y$$
> 
> Substitute $E[X] = \mu_X$ and $E[Y] = \mu_Y$:
> 
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - \mu_Y \mu_X - \mu_X \mu_Y + \mu_X \mu_Y = E[XY] - \mu_X \mu_Y$$
> 
> $$\therefore \text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] \quad \text{(Proved)}$$

## 3.2 Covariance of Independent Variables

> [!note]- View Proof: Theorem for Independent Variables ($\text{Cov}(X,Y) = 0$)
> **Theorem:** If two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0$.
> 
> **Proof:**
> For independent random variables $X$ and $Y$, the joint PDF factors into the product of marginal PDFs: $f(x, y) = g(x) h(y)$.
> Consequently, the joint expectation factors as:
> 
> $$E[XY] = E[X] E[Y] = \mu_X \mu_Y$$
> 
> Substituting this into the operational covariance formula:
> 
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - \mu_X \mu_Y = \mu_X \mu_Y - \mu_X \mu_Y = 0 \quad \text{(Proved)}$$

> [!warning] Exam Caution: The Converse is NOT Always True
> While independence guarantees $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0$, **$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0$ does NOT imply independence**. Zero covariance only indicates the absence of a *linear* relationship; non-linear dependencies may still exist between $X$ and $Y$.

## 3.3 Algebraic Properties of Covariance

> [!note]- View Proofs: Algebraic Properties of Covariance
> 
> #### Property 1: Self-Covariance equals Variance
> $$\text{Cov}(X, X) = \text{Var}(X)$$
> *Proof:* $\text{Cov}(X, X) = E[(X - \mu_X)(X - \mu_X)] = E[(X - \mu_X)^2] = \text{Var}(X)$.
> 
> #### Property 2: Symmetry
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \text{Cov}(Y, X)$$
> *Proof:* $E[(X - \mu_X)(Y - \mu_Y)] = E[(Y - \mu_Y)(X - \mu_X)] = \text{Cov}(Y, X)$.
> 
> #### Property 3: Scalar Multiplication
> $$\text{Cov}(aX, bY) = ab \cdot \text{Cov}(X, Y)$$
> *Proof:* 
> $\text{Cov}(aX, bY) = E[(aX - a\mu_X)(bY - b\mu_Y)] = E[a(X - \mu_X) b(Y - \mu_Y)] = ab E[(X - \mu_X)(Y - \mu_Y)] = ab \cdot \text{Cov}(X, Y)$.
> 
> #### Property 4: Additivity / Linearity
> $$\text{Cov}(X + Y, Z) = \text{Cov}(X, Z) + \text{Cov}(Y, Z)$$
> *Proof:*
> $$\text{Cov}(X + Y, Z) = E\left[ \left( (X+Y) - (\mu_X+\mu_Y) \right)(Z - \mu_Z) \right]$$
> $$= E\left[ \left( (X - \mu_X) + (Y - \mu_Y) \right)(Z - \mu_Z) \right]$$
> $$= E\left[ (X - \mu_X)(Z - \mu_Z) \right] + E\left[ (Y - \mu_Y)(Z - \mu_Z) \right] = \text{Cov}(X, Z) + \text{Cov}(Y, Z)$$

## 3.4 Variance of Sum Theorem

> [!note]- View Proof: Variance of Sum Theorem
> **Theorem:** For any set of random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$:
> 
> $$\text{Var}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{Var}(X_i) + 2 \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \text{Cov}(X_i, X_j)$$
> 
> **Proof for Two Variables ($n = 2$):**
> We want to prove $\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y) + 2 \text{Cov}(X, Y)$.
> 
> Using Property 1 (Self-Covariance) and Property 4 (Additivity):
> 
> $$\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Cov}(X + Y, X + Y)$$
> $$= \text{Cov}(X, X + Y) + \text{Cov}(Y, X + Y)$$
> $$= \text{Cov}(X, X) + \text{Cov}(X, Y) + \text{Cov}(Y, X) + \text{Cov}(Y, Y)$$
> 
> Since $\text{Cov}(X, X) = \text{Var}(X)$, $\text{Cov}(Y, Y) = \text{Var}(Y)$, and $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \text{Cov}(Y, X)$:
> 
> $$\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y) + 2 \text{Cov}(X, Y) \quad \text{(Proved)}$$

> [!info] Special Case: Independent Variables
> If $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ are mutually independent, then $\text{Cov}(X_i, X_j) = 0$ for all $i \neq j$.
> Thus, the variance of the sum simplifies directly to the sum of variances:
> 
> $$\text{Var}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{Var}(X_i)$$

## 3.5 Continuous Joint PDF Worked Problem

### General Methodology for Joint Density Problems
Given a joint continuous probability density function $f(x, y)$:
1. **Marginal PDF of $X$:** $g(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x, y) \, dy$
2. **Marginal PDF of $Y$:** $h(y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x, y) \, dx$
3. **Expectation $E[X]$:** $E[X] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x g(x) \, dx$
4. **Expectation $E[Y]$:** $E[Y] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y h(y) \, dy$
5. **Joint Expectation $E[XY]$:** $E[XY] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x y f(x, y) \, dx \, dy$
6. **Covariance:** $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X] E[Y]$

---

### Solved Practice Problem
**Question:** Given the continuous joint PDF:

$$f(x, y) = \begin{cases} x + y, & 0 \le x \le 1, \, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Find:
1. Marginal PDFs $g(x)$ and $h(y)$
2. Expectations $E[X]$ and $E[Y]$
3. Joint expectation $E[XY]$
4. Covariance $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$
5. Correlation coefficient $\rho(X, Y)$

> [!note]- View Complete Step-by-Step Solution
> **Step 1: Compute Marginal PDFs $g(x)$ and $h(y)$**
> $$g(x) = \int_{0}^{1} (x + y) \, dy = \left[ xy + \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{1} = x + \frac{1}{2}, \quad 0 \le x \le 1$$
> By symmetry, $h(y) = y + \frac{1}{2}, \quad 0 \le y \le 1$.
> 
> **Step 2: Compute $E[X]$ and $E[Y]$**
> $$E[X] = \int_{0}^{1} x \left( x + \frac{1}{2} \right) dx = \int_{0}^{1} \left( x^2 + \frac{x}{2} \right) dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{x^2}{4} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{12}$$
> By symmetry, $E[Y] = \frac{7}{12}$.
> 
> **Step 3: Compute Joint Expectation $E[XY]$**
> $$E[XY] = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} xy(x + y) \, dx \, dy = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} (x^2 y + x y^2) \, dx \, dy$$
> Inner integral w.r.t $x$: $\int_{0}^{1} (x^2 y + x y^2) \, dx = \left[ \frac{x^3 y}{3} + \frac{x^2 y^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{y}{3} + \frac{y^2}{2}$
> Outer integral w.r.t $y$: $\int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{y}{3} + \frac{y^2}{2} \right) dy = \left[ \frac{y^2}{6} + \frac{y^3}{6} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$
> 
> **Step 4: Compute Covariance $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$**
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X] E[Y] = \frac{1}{3} - \left( \frac{7}{12} \right) \left( \frac{7}{12} \right) = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{49}{144} = \frac{48 - 49}{144} = -\frac{1}{144} \approx -0.00694$$
> 
> **Step 5: Compute Variances and Correlation Coefficient $\rho(X, Y)$**
> $$E[X^2] = \int_{0}^{1} x^2 \left( x + \frac{1}{2} \right) dx = \int_{0}^{1} \left( x^3 + \frac{x^2}{2} \right) dx = \left[ \frac{x^4}{4} + \frac{x^3}{6} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{12}$$
> $$V[X] = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 = \frac{5}{12} - \left( \frac{7}{12} \right)^2 = \frac{5}{12} - \frac{49}{144} = \frac{60 - 49}{144} = \frac{11}{144}$$
> By symmetry, $V[Y] = \frac{11}{144}$. Thus $\sigma_X \sigma_Y = \sqrt{\frac{11}{144}} \sqrt{\frac{11}{144}} = \frac{11}{144}$.
> 
> $$\rho(X, Y) = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y} = \frac{-1/144}{11/144} = -\frac{1}{11} \approx -0.0909$$

## 3.6 Pearson's Correlation Coefficient ($\rho$)

### Definition & Formula
Let $X$ and $Y$ be two random variables with finite non-zero variances $\text{Var}(X)$ and $\text{Var}(Y)$. The **Pearson Correlation Coefficient** $\rho(X, Y)$ is defined as:

$$\rho(X, Y) = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(X) \cdot \text{Var}(Y)}} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y}$$

> [!important] Special Definition Rule
> If $\text{Var}(X) = 0$ or $\text{Var}(Y) = 0$, the correlation coefficient is defined to be zero ($\rho(X, Y) = 0$).

### Key Properties of Correlation Coefficient ($\rho$)
1. **Dimensionless & Scale Invariance:** $\rho(X, Y)$ remains **unaffected by changes of origin or unit scale**. If $U = \frac{X - a}{c}$ and $V = \frac{Y - b}{d}$, then $|\rho(U, V)| = |\rho(X, Y)|$.
2. **Bounded Range:** The value of $\rho$ always lies strictly between $-1$ and $+1$:
   $$-1 \le \rho(X, Y) \le +1$$

### Visual & Physical Interpretation

```
      Negative Correlation               Zero Correlation               Positive Correlation
          (ρ = -1)                           (ρ = 0)                          (ρ = +1)
          
        Y | *                              Y |   *    *                 Y |          *
          |   *                              | *    *                     |        *
          |     *                            |   *    *                   |      *
          |       *                          | *    *                     |    *
          +---------- X                      +---------- X                +---------- X
```

- **$\rho = +1$ (Perfect Positive Correlation):** Variables move together in exact linear proportion with a positive slope.
- **$\rho = -1$ (Perfect Negative Correlation):** Variables move in exact opposite directions with a negative slope.
- **$\rho = 0$ (No Correlation):** No linear relationship exists between $X$ and $Y$.
