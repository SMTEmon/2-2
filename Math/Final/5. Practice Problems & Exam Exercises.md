## 📝 Problem 1: Binomial Distribution & Diagnostic Constraints
A 5-question multiple-choice quiz has 4 choices per question ($p = 0.25$). A student completely guesses on all $n = 5$ questions.

1. **(a)** Find the probability of getting **exactly 2 questions correct**.
2. **(b)** Compute the Mean $E[X]$ and Variance $V[X]$.
3. **(c)** *Exam Diagnostic:* Can a Binomial distribution have Mean $= 6$ and Standard Deviation $= 3$? Explain mathematically.

> [!note]- View Complete Solution
> **(a) Binomial PMF for $x = 2$:**
> $$P(X = 2) = \binom{5}{2} (0.25)^2 (0.75)^3 = 10 \times 0.0625 \times 0.421875 = \mathbf{0.2637 \quad (26.37\%)}$$
> 
> **(b) Mean and Variance:**
> - Mean: $E[X] = np = 5 \times 0.25 = \mathbf{1.25}$
> - Variance: $V[X] = npq = 5 \times 0.25 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{0.9375}$
> 
> **(c) Diagnostic Rule Check:**
> - Standard Deviation $= 3 \implies \text{Variance } V[X] = 3^2 = 9$.
> - For Binomial, $q = \frac{V[X]}{E[X]} = \frac{9}{6} = 1.5$.
> - Since probability $q$ cannot exceed $1.0$, **this is mathematically impossible!** (Variance must *always* be less than Mean for Binomial).

## 📝 Problem 2: Poisson Distribution
A fast-food drive-thru receives an average of $\mu = 4$ cars every 5 minutes.

1. **(a)** What is the probability that **no cars ($X = 0$)** arrive in a 5-minute period?
2. **(b)** What is the probability that **at least 2 cars ($X \ge 2$)** arrive in a 5-minute period?

> [!note]- View Complete Solution
> **(a) Probability of $X = 0$:**
> $$P(X = 0) = \frac{e^{-4} \cdot 4^0}{0!} = e^{-4} \approx \mathbf{0.0183 \quad (1.83\%)}$$
> 
> **(b) Probability of $X \ge 2$:**
> $$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - [P(X = 0) + P(X = 1)]$$
> $$P(X = 1) = \frac{e^{-4} \cdot 4^1}{1!} = 4 e^{-4} \approx 0.0733$$
> $$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - [0.0183 + 0.0733] = 1 - 0.0916 = \mathbf{0.9084 \quad (90.84\%)}$$

## 📝 Problem 3: Covariance & Variance of a Sum
Two investment stocks $X$ and $Y$ have the following statistics:
- $E[X] = 10, \, E[Y] = 15$
- $\text{Var}(X) = 16, \, \text{Var}(Y) = 25$
- Joint expectation $E[XY] = 162$

1. **(a)** Calculate the Covariance $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$.
2. **(b)** Calculate the total Variance of the combined portfolio $\text{Var}(X + Y)$.

> [!note]- View Complete Solution
> **(a) Covariance:**
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] = 162 - (10 \times 15) = 162 - 150 = \mathbf{12}$$
> 
> **(b) Variance of Sum:**
> $$\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y) + 2\text{Cov}(X, Y)$$
> $$\text{Var}(X + Y) = 16 + 25 + 2(12) = 41 + 24 = \mathbf{65}$$

## 📝 Problem 4: Pearson’s Correlation Coefficient ($\rho$)
Using the values from Problem 3 ($\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 12$, $\text{Var}(X) = 16$, $\text{Var}(Y) = 25$):

1. **(a)** Calculate Pearson's Correlation Coefficient $\rho(X, Y)$.
2. **(b)** Interpret the direction and strength of the correlation.

> [!note]- View Complete Solution
> **(a) Correlation Coefficient:**
> $$\sigma_X = \sqrt{16} = 4, \quad \sigma_Y = \sqrt{25} = 5$$
> $$\rho(X, Y) = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \cdot \sigma_Y} = \frac{12}{4 \times 5} = \frac{12}{20} = \mathbf{+0.60}$$
> 
> **(b) Interpretation:**
> $\rho = +0.60$ indicates a **moderate-to-strong positive linear relationship** (as stock $X$ rises, stock $Y$ tends to rise).

## 📝 Problem 5: Chebyshev’s Inequality
A city's daily electricity usage has a mean of $\mu = 500 \text{ MW}$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 50 \text{ MW}$. The distribution shape is **unknown**.

Use Chebyshev's inequality to find the **minimum probability** that electricity usage on any given day will be between **$400 \text{ MW}$ and $600 \text{ MW}$**.

> [!note]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Find distance from mean ($\mu = 500$):**
> Distance $= 600 - 500 = 100 \text{ MW}$.
> 
> **Step 2: Calculate $k$:**
> $$k = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\sigma} = \frac{100}{50} = 2$$
> 
> **Step 3: Apply Chebyshev's formula:**
> $$\text{Minimum Probability} = 1 - \frac{1}{k^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{2^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \mathbf{0.75 \quad (75\%)}$$

## 📝 Problem 6: Normal Distribution & Reading the $Z$-Table
Test scores follow a normal distribution $N(\mu = 70, \, \sigma^2 = 100)$, so $\sigma = 10$.

1. **(a)** Find the probability that a student scores **$84.2$ or less** ($P(X \le 84.2)$).
2. **(b)** What cutoff score is required to be in the **top 5%** of the class ($95^{\text{th}}$ percentile)?

> [!note]- View Complete Solution
> **(a) Convert to $Z$-score:**
> $$Z = \frac{84.2 - 70}{10} = \frac{14.2}{10} = \mathbf{1.42}$$
> Look up $Z = 1.42$ on the Positive $Z$-table (Row `1.4`, Column `.02`):
> $$P(X \le 84.2) = P(Z \le 1.42) = \mathbf{0.9222 \quad (92.22\%)}$$
> 
> **(b) Top 5% (Cumulative area = $0.9500$):**
> Look up area `.9500` inside the table $\implies Z = \mathbf{1.645}$.
> Convert $Z$ back to score $X$:
> $$X = \mu + Z\sigma = 70 + (1.645 \times 10) = 70 + 16.45 = \mathbf{86.45}$$
> Cutoff score required is **$86.45$**.
