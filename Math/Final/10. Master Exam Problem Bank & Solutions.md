## Module 1: Binomial Distribution

### Type 1: Direct Binomial Probability Calculations
**Problem 1.1 (Serum Inoculation):**  
It is known that $90\%$ of mice inoculated with a protective serum are protected against a deadly virus. If 5 inoculated mice are exposed to the virus ($n = 5$):
1. What is the probability that exactly 3 mice contract the virus?
2. What is the probability that at least 2 mice contract the virus?

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> Let $X$ be the number of mice that **contract the virus**.
> - Success in this context = contracting the virus $\implies p = 1 - 0.90 = 0.10$.
> - Failure (protection) $\implies q = 0.90$.
> - Sample size $n = 5$.
> - $X \sim B(5, 0.10)$.
> 
> **Part (i): Exactly 3 mice contract the virus ($X = 3$)**
> $$P(X = 3) = \binom{5}{3} p^3 q^{5-3} = \binom{5}{3} (0.10)^3 (0.90)^2$$
> $$P(X = 3) = 10 \times 0.001 \times 0.81 = \mathbf{0.0081}$$
> 
> **Part (ii): At least 2 mice contract the virus ($X \ge 2$)**
> Using the complement rule:
> $$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - [P(X = 0) + P(X = 1)]$$
> 
> $$P(X = 0) = \binom{5}{0} (0.10)^0 (0.90)^5 = (0.90)^5 = 0.59049$$
> $$P(X = 1) = \binom{5}{1} (0.10)^1 (0.90)^4 = 5 \times 0.10 \times 0.6561 = 0.32805$$
> 
> $$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - (0.59049 + 0.32805) = 1 - 0.91854 = \mathbf{0.08146}$$
> 
> **Answer:** (i) $0.0081$, (ii) $0.0815$.

### Type 2: Parameter Identification & Validity Checking
**Problem 1.2 (Diagnostic Check):**  
A student in an industrial quality lab reports that a sample of components follows a Binomial distribution with Mean $\mu = 8$ and Standard Deviation $\sigma = 3$. Determine whether this report is mathematically valid.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> For a Binomial distribution:
> $$\text{Mean} = np = 8$$
> $$\text{Variance} = npq = \sigma^2 = 3^2 = 9$$
> 
> Dividing variance by mean:
> $$\frac{npq}{np} = q \implies q = \frac{9}{8} = 1.125$$
> 
> Since $q$ is a probability, it must strictly satisfy $0 \le q \le 1$. Here $q = 1.125 > 1$ (and consequently $p = 1 - q = -0.125 < 0$).
> 
> In addition, for any Binomial distribution, $\text{Var}(X) = npq < np = E[X]$ because $0 < q < 1$. Here $\text{Variance } (9) > \text{Mean } (8)$, which directly contradicts the Binomial variance condition.
> 
> **Answer:** The report is **mathematically impossible and invalid**.

### Type 3: Fitting Theoretical Binomial Frequencies (Recurrence Relation)
**Problem 1.3 (Coin Tossing Experiment):**  
Five fair-looking coins are tossed simultaneously, and the experiment is repeated $N = 200$ times. The observed distribution of the number of heads is:

| Number of Heads ($x$) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Total ($N$) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Observed Frequency ($f$)** | 12 | 56 | 74 | 39 | 18 | 1 | **200** |

Fit a theoretical Binomial distribution to this data using the **Binomial Recurrence Relation** and determine the expected frequencies.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Compute the Sample Mean $\bar{x}$**
> $$\sum f x = (0 \times 12) + (1 \times 56) + (2 \times 74) + (3 \times 39) + (4 \times 18) + (5 \times 1)$$
> $$\sum f x = 0 + 56 + 148 + 117 + 72 + 5 = 398$$
> $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f x}{N} = \frac{398}{200} = 1.99$$
> 
> **Step 2: Estimate Parameters $p$ and $q$**
> Equating $\bar{x} = np$ with $n = 5$:
> $$5p = 1.99 \implies p = \frac{1.99}{5} = \mathbf{0.398}$$
> $$q = 1 - p = 1 - 0.398 = \mathbf{0.602}$$
> Ratio $\frac{p}{q} = \frac{0.398}{0.602} \approx \mathbf{0.66113}$
> 
> **Step 3: Calculate Base Term $b(0)$**
> $$b(0; 5, 0.398) = q^5 = (0.602)^5 \approx \mathbf{0.07907}$$
> 
> **Step 4: Use Binomial Recurrence Formula $b(x+1) = \left(\frac{n-x}{x+1}\right)\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)b(x)$**
> - $x = 0: \quad b(1) = \left(\frac{5-0}{1}\right)(0.66113)(0.07907) = 5 \times 0.66113 \times 0.07907 = \mathbf{0.26136}$
> - $x = 1: \quad b(2) = \left(\frac{5-1}{2}\right)(0.66113)(0.26136) = 2 \times 0.66113 \times 0.26136 = \mathbf{0.34559}$
> - $x = 2: \quad b(3) = \left(\frac{5-2}{3}\right)(0.66113)(0.34559) = 1 \times 0.66113 \times 0.34559 = \mathbf{0.22847}$
> - $x = 3: \quad b(4) = \left(\frac{5-3}{4}\right)(0.66113)(0.22847) = 0.5 \times 0.66113 \times 0.22847 = \mathbf{0.07553}$
> - $x = 4: \quad b(5) = \left(\frac{5-4}{5}\right)(0.66113)(0.07553) = 0.2 \times 0.66113 \times 0.07553 = \mathbf{0.00998}$
> 
> **Step 5: Compute Expected Frequencies $E_x = N \times b(x) = 200 \times b(x)$**
> - $E_0 = 200 \times 0.07907 = 15.81 \approx \mathbf{16}$
> - $E_1 = 200 \times 0.26136 = 52.27 \approx \mathbf{52}$
> - $E_2 = 200 \times 0.34559 = 69.12 \approx \mathbf{69}$
> - $E_3 = 200 \times 0.22847 = 45.69 \approx \mathbf{46}$
> - $E_4 = 200 \times 0.07553 = 15.11 \approx \mathbf{15}$
> - $E_5 = 200 \times 0.00998 = 2.00 \approx \mathbf{2}$
> 
> **Comparison Table:**
> 
> | $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Total |
> |:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
> | **Observed $f$** | 12 | 56 | 74 | 39 | 18 | 1 | **200** |
> | **Expected $E_x$** | 16 | 52 | 69 | 46 | 15 | 2 | **200** |

## Module 2: Poisson Distribution

### Type 4: Interval Rescaling & Rate Computation
**Problem 2.1 (Telephone Switchboard):**  
Telephone calls arrive at a central university switchboard at an average rate of $0.6$ calls per minute.
1. Find the probability that exactly 3 calls arrive in a 5-minute interval.
2. Find the probability that at least 1 call arrives during a 2-minute interval.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Part (i): Exactly 3 calls in 5 minutes**
> First rescale the parameter $\lambda$ for $t = 5$ minutes:
> $$\lambda = 0.6 \times 5 = 3.0 \text{ calls}$$
> Now compute $P(X = 3)$ using the Poisson PMF:
> $$P(X = 3) = \frac{e^{-3} (3)^3}{3!} = \frac{e^{-3} \times 27}{6} = 4.5 \times e^{-3}$$
> Since $e^{-3} \approx 0.049787$:
> $$P(X = 3) = 4.5 \times 0.049787 = \mathbf{0.2240}$$
> 
> **Part (ii): At least 1 call in 2 minutes**
> Rescale $\lambda$ for $t = 2$ minutes:
> $$\lambda = 0.6 \times 2 = 1.2 \text{ calls}$$
> $$P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - \frac{e^{-1.2} (1.2)^0}{0!} = 1 - e^{-1.2}$$
> Since $e^{-1.2} \approx 0.30119$:
> $$P(X \ge 1) = 1 - 0.30119 = \mathbf{0.6988}$$
> 
> **Answer:** (i) $0.2240$, (ii) $0.6988$.

### Type 5: Successive Terms via Poisson Recurrence
**Problem 2.2 (Poisson Recurrence Calculation):**  
A Poisson variate $X$ has mean $\lambda = 2$. Given that $P(X = 0) = e^{-2} \approx 0.13534$, use the **Poisson Recurrence Relation** to find $P(X = 1)$, $P(X = 2)$, $P(X = 3)$, and $P(X = 4)$.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> The Poisson recurrence relation is:
> $$f(x+1) = \left(\frac{\lambda}{x+1}\right) f(x)$$
> Given $\lambda = 2$ and $f(0) = 0.13534$:
> - $x = 0: \quad f(1) = \left(\frac{2}{1}\right) f(0) = 2 \times 0.13534 = \mathbf{0.27068}$
> - $x = 1: \quad f(2) = \left(\frac{2}{2}\right) f(1) = 1 \times 0.27068 = \mathbf{0.27068}$
> - $x = 2: \quad f(3) = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right) f(2) = \frac{2}{3} \times 0.27068 = \mathbf{0.18045}$
> - $x = 3: \quad f(4) = \left(\frac{2}{4}\right) f(3) = 0.5 \times 0.18045 = \mathbf{0.09023}$
> 
> **Answer:** $P(1) = 0.2707, P(2) = 0.2707, P(3) = 0.1805, P(4) = 0.0902$.

### Type 6: Fitting Poisson Distribution to Observed Data
**Problem 2.3 (Accident Data Fitting):**  
The number of road accidents per day recorded over $N = 100$ days is given below:

| Accidents per day ($x$) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Total |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Observed Days ($f$)** | 40 | 35 | 18 | 5 | 2 | **100** |

Fit a Poisson distribution to this data and calculate the expected frequencies.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Compute Mean $\lambda$**
> $$\sum f x = (0 \times 40) + (1 \times 35) + (2 \times 18) + (3 \times 5) + (4 \times 2) = 0 + 35 + 36 + 15 + 8 = 94$$
> $$\lambda = \bar{x} = \frac{\sum f x}{N} = \frac{94}{100} = \mathbf{0.94}$$
> 
> **Step 2: Base Term $f(0)$**
> $$f(0) = e^{-\lambda} = e^{-0.94} \approx \mathbf{0.3906}$$
> 
> **Step 3: Recursive Probabilities $f(x+1) = \frac{0.94}{x+1} f(x)$**
> - $f(1) = \left(\frac{0.94}{1}\right)(0.3906) = \mathbf{0.3672}$
> - $f(2) = \left(\frac{0.94}{2}\right)(0.3672) = 0.47 \times 0.3672 = \mathbf{0.1726}$
> - $f(3) = \left(\frac{0.94}{3}\right)(0.1726) = \mathbf{0.0541}$
> - $f(4) = \left(\frac{0.94}{4}\right)(0.0541) = \mathbf{0.0127}$
> 
> **Step 4: Expected Frequencies $E_x = 100 \times f(x)$**
> - $E_0 = 100 \times 0.3906 \approx \mathbf{39}$
> - $E_1 = 100 \times 0.3672 \approx \mathbf{37}$
> - $E_2 = 100 \times 0.1726 \approx \mathbf{17}$
> - $E_3 = 100 \times 0.0541 \approx \mathbf{5}$
> - $E_4 = 100 \times 0.0127 \approx \mathbf{1}$ (and $\ge 4$ carries remaining 1 day)
> 
> **Answer:** Expected frequencies: $E_0=39, E_1=37, E_2=17, E_3=5, E_4=2$.

## Module 3: Continuous Uniform Distribution

### Type 7: Rectangular Distribution Properties & Probabilities
**Problem 3.1 (Bus Waiting Time):**  
A city bus arrives at a stop uniformly distributed between 8:00 AM and 8:20 AM. Let $X$ denote the arrival time in minutes past 8:00 AM ($a = 0, b = 20$).
1. Write down the PDF $f(x)$ and CDF $F(x)$.
2. What is the mean arrival time and the standard deviation?
3. What is the probability that a passenger who arrives at 8:05 AM waits less than 7 minutes for the bus?

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> Here $a = 0$ and $b = 20 \implies b - a = 20$.
> 
> **Part (i): PDF and CDF**
> $$f(x) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{20} = 0.05, & 0 \le x \le 20 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
> 
> $$F(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & x < 0 \\ \frac{x}{20}, & 0 \le x \le 20 \\ 1, & x > 20 \end{cases}$$
> 
> **Part (ii): Mean and Standard Deviation**
> $$\mu = E[X] = \frac{a + b}{2} = \frac{0 + 20}{2} = \mathbf{10 \text{ minutes}}$$
> $$\sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X) = \frac{(b - a)^2}{12} = \frac{20^2}{12} = \frac{400}{12} = \frac{100}{3} \approx 33.333$$
> $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{100}{3}} = \frac{10}{\sqrt{3}} \approx \mathbf{5.774 \text{ minutes}}$$
> 
> **Part (iii): Passenger waiting less than 7 minutes**
> The passenger arrives at $x = 5$. Waiting less than 7 minutes means the bus arrives between $x = 5$ and $x = 5 + 7 = 12$:
> $$P(5 \le X \le 12) = F(12) - F(5) = \frac{12}{20} - \frac{5}{20} = \frac{7}{20} = \mathbf{0.35}$$
> 
> **Answer:** (i) $f(x) = 1/20$, (ii) $\mu = 10, \sigma = 5.774$, (iii) $0.35$.

## Module 4: Exponential Distribution

### Type 8: Lifespan & Rate Calculations
**Problem 4.1 (Transistor Lifespan):**  
The lifespan $X$ of an industrial computer transistor is exponentially distributed with a mean life of 1,000 hours.
1. What is the rate parameter $\lambda$?
2. Find the probability that a transistor burns out within the first 400 hours.
3. Find the probability that a transistor lasts longer than 1,500 hours.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Part (i): Rate Parameter $\lambda$**
> For an exponential distribution, $\text{Mean} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = 1000 \implies \lambda = \frac{1}{1000} = \mathbf{0.001 \text{ hr}^{-1}}$.
> The PDF is $f(x) = 0.001 e^{-0.001 x}$ for $x \ge 0$.
> 
> **Part (ii): Burns out within 400 hours ($X \le 400$)**
> Use the CDF $F(x) = 1 - e^{-\lambda x}$:
> $$P(X \le 400) = 1 - e^{-(0.001)(400)} = 1 - e^{-0.4}$$
> Since $e^{-0.4} \approx 0.67032$:
> $$P(X \le 400) = 1 - 0.67032 = \mathbf{0.3297}$$
> 
> **Part (iii): Lasts longer than 1,500 hours ($X > 1500$)**
> Use the Survival Function $P(X > x) = e^{-\lambda x}$:
> $$P(X > 1500) = e^{-(0.001)(1500)} = e^{-1.5} \approx \mathbf{0.2231}$$
> 
> **Answer:** (i) $\lambda = 0.001$, (ii) $0.3297$, (iii) $0.2231$.

### Type 9: Applying the Memoryless Property
**Problem 4.2 (Memoryless Server Repair):**  
The time required to repair a server follows an exponential distribution with an average of 4 hours ($\lambda = 0.25$). If a technician has already spent 3 hours trying to repair the server without success, what is the probability that the total repair time will exceed 7 hours?

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> We are asked for the conditional probability $P(X > 7 \mid X > 3)$.
> We can write $7 = 3 + 4$, where $s = 3$ and $t = 4$.
> 
> By the **Memoryless Property of the Exponential Distribution**:
> $$P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$$
> 
> Therefore:
> $$P(X > 3 + 4 \mid X > 3) = P(X > 4)$$
> 
> Using $P(X > t) = e^{-\lambda t}$ with $\lambda = 0.25$ and $t = 4$:
> $$P(X > 4) = e^{-(0.25)(4)} = e^{-1} \approx \mathbf{0.3679}$$
> 
> **Answer:** $\mathbf{0.3679}$ (or approximately $36.8\%$).

## Module 5: Chebyshev's Inequality

### Type 10: Lower Bound on Arbitrary Distributions
**Problem 5.1 (Production Output Quality):**  
A manufacturing line produces steel rods with an average length of $\mu = 50\text{ cm}$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 2\text{ cm}$. The exact shape of the probability distribution of rod lengths is completely unknown.
1. What is the minimum probability that a randomly chosen rod has a length between $44\text{ cm}$ and $56\text{ cm}$?
2. What is the maximum probability that a rod deviates from the mean by at least $6\text{ cm}$?

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Part (i): Between 44 cm and 56 cm**
> Notice the interval: $44 < X < 56 \implies 50 - 6 < X < 50 + 6 \implies |X - 50| < 6$.
> Express the deviation in terms of standard deviation $\sigma = 2$:
> $$k\sigma = 6 \implies k(2) = 6 \implies k = 3$$
> 
> Applying Chebyshev's Inequality:
> $$P(|X - \mu| < k\sigma) \ge 1 - \frac{1}{k^2}$$
> $$P(|X - 50| < 3\sigma) \ge 1 - \frac{1}{3^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{9} = \frac{8}{9} \approx \mathbf{0.8889}$$
> **Answer:** At least **$88.89\%$**.
> 
> **Part (ii): Deviates by at least 6 cm ($|X - 50| \ge 6$)**
> This is the tail probability with $k = 3$:
> $$P(|X - \mu| \ge k\sigma) \le \frac{1}{k^2}$$
> $$P(|X - 50| \ge 6) \le \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9} \approx \mathbf{0.1111}$$
> **Answer:** At most **$11.11\%$**.

### Type 11: Chebyshev vs Exact Integration Comparison
**Problem 5.2 (Beta Density Comparison from Slides):**  
A random variable $X$ has the continuous density function:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 630 x^4 (1-x)^4, & 0 < x < 1 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
Given that $\mu = 0.5$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{44}} \approx 0.15$:
1. Determine the Chebyshev lower bound for $k = 2$.
2. State the exact probability interval and compare it with the bound.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Part (i): Chebyshev Lower Bound ($k = 2$)**
> $$P(|X - \mu| < 2\sigma) \ge 1 - \frac{1}{2^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \mathbf{0.75}$$
> 
> **Part (ii): Interval & Exact Comparison**
> - Lower limit: $\mu - 2\sigma = 0.5 - 2(0.15) = 0.20$
> - Upper limit: $\mu + 2\sigma = 0.5 + 2(0.15) = 0.80$
> 
> Exact integration:
> $$P(0.20 < X < 0.80) = \int_{0.2}^{0.8} 630 x^4 (1-x)^4 dx = \mathbf{0.96}$$
> 
> **Comparison:** The exact probability ($0.96$) is significantly higher than the conservative lower bound ($0.75$) guaranteed by Chebyshev's inequality.

## Module 6: Normal Distribution

### Type 12: Standardization & $Z$-Table Lookup
**Problem 6.1 (Exam Scores):**  
Final exam marks in Math 4441 are normally distributed with mean $\mu = 68$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 10$.
1. What percentage of students score above 85?
2. What percentage of students score between 60 and 80?

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Part (i): Score above 85 ($X > 85$)**
> Standardize $X$ to $Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$:
> $$Z = \frac{85 - 68}{10} = \frac{17}{10} = 1.70$$
> $$P(X > 85) = P(Z > 1.70) = 1 - \Phi(1.70)$$
> From standard normal tables, $\Phi(1.70) = 0.9554$:
> $$P(X > 85) = 1 - 0.9554 = 0.0446 \implies \mathbf{4.46\%}$$
> 
> **Part (ii): Score between 60 and 80 ($60 < X < 80$)**
> Standardize both boundaries:
> $$Z_1 = \frac{60 - 68}{10} = \frac{-8}{10} = -0.80$$
> $$Z_2 = \frac{80 - 68}{10} = \frac{12}{10} = 1.20$$
> 
> $$P(60 < X < 80) = P(-0.80 < Z < 1.20) = \Phi(1.20) - \Phi(-0.80)$$
> Using symmetry $\Phi(-0.80) = 1 - \Phi(0.80)$:
> - $\Phi(1.20) = 0.8849$
> - $\Phi(0.80) = 0.7881 \implies \Phi(-0.80) = 1 - 0.7881 = 0.2119$
> 
> $$P(60 < X < 80) = 0.8849 - 0.2119 = 0.6730 \implies \mathbf{67.30\%}$$
> 
> **Answer:** (i) $4.46\%$, (ii) $67.30\%$.

### Type 13: Central Area Symmetrical Interval
**Problem 6.2 (Classroom Problem):**  
In a normal distribution with mean $\mu = 50$ and variance $\sigma^2 = 100$ ($\sigma = 10$), find the values of $X$ that cover the **central $50\%$** of the area under the normal curve.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> By symmetry, the central $50\%$ area lies between $-z$ and $+z$ such that:
> $$P(-z < Z < z) = 0.50 \implies 2 P(0 < Z < z) = 0.50 \implies P(0 < Z < z) = 0.25$$
> 
> In terms of cumulative CDF:
> $$\Phi(z) = 0.50 + 0.25 = 0.75$$
> 
> From standard normal Z-tables, the $Z$-value for probability $0.7500$ is:
> $$z \approx 0.6745 \approx \mathbf{0.675}$$
> *(Note: Classroom note page 19 also references $z \approx 0.599$ for specific tabular approximations; standard value is $0.675$).*
> 
> Transforming back to $X = \mu \pm z\sigma$:
> - Lower bound: $x_1 = 50 - (0.675 \times 10) = 50 - 6.75 = \mathbf{43.25}$
> - Upper bound: $x_2 = 50 + (0.675 \times 10) = 50 + 6.75 = \mathbf{56.75}$
> 
> **Answer:** The central $50\%$ is covered by the interval **$(43.25, 56.75)$**.

### Type 14: Inverse Normal to Find Unknown Parameter
**Problem 6.3 (Unknown Variance):**  
A normal random variable $X$ has mean $\mu = 30$ and unknown variance $\sigma^2$. Given that $P(X < 35) = 0.6915$, find the standard deviation $\sigma$ and variance $\sigma^2$.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> Standardize the inequality:
> $$P(X < 35) = P\left(Z < \frac{35 - 30}{\sigma}\right) = P\left(Z < \frac{5}{\sigma}\right) = 0.6915$$
> 
> Look up $0.6915$ in the standard normal table:
> $$\Phi(0.50) = 0.6915$$
> 
> Equating the $Z$-scores:
> $$\frac{5}{\sigma} = 0.50 \implies \sigma = \frac{5}{0.50} = \mathbf{10}$$
> 
> Variance:
> $$\sigma^2 = 10^2 = \mathbf{100}$$
> 
> **Answer:** Standard deviation $\sigma = 10$, Variance $\sigma^2 = 100$.

## Module 7: Covariance & Correlation

### Type 15: Continuous Joint PDF Analysis
**Problem 7.1 (Classroom Lecture Problem):**  
Consider the continuous joint PDF:
$$f(x, y) = \begin{cases} \frac{6}{5}(x^2 + 2xy), & 0 \le x \le 1, \, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
1. Find the marginal density functions $g(x)$ and $h(y)$.
2. Compute expectations $E[X]$, $E[Y]$, and $E[XY]$.
3. Compute Covariance $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$.
4. Determine the correlation coefficient $\rho(X, Y)$.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Marginals**
> $$g(x) = \int_0^1 \frac{6}{5}(x^2 + 2xy) dy = \frac{6}{5}\left[x^2 y + xy^2\right]_0^1 = \mathbf{\frac{6}{5}x(x + 1)}$$
> $$h(y) = \int_0^1 \frac{6}{5}(x^2 + 2xy) dx = \frac{6}{5}\left[\frac{x^3}{3} + x^2 y\right]_0^1 = \mathbf{\frac{2}{5}(1 + 3y)}$$
> 
> **Step 2: Expectations**
> $$E[X] = \int_0^1 x \cdot \frac{6}{5}(x^2 + x) dx = \frac{6}{5}\left[\frac{x^4}{4} + \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \frac{6}{5}\left(\frac{7}{12}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{7}{10} = 0.7}$$
> $$E[Y] = \int_0^1 y \cdot \frac{2}{5}(1 + 3y) dy = \frac{2}{5}\left[\frac{y^2}{2} + y^3\right]_0^1 = \frac{2}{5}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{3}{5} = 0.6}$$
> $$E[XY] = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 xy \cdot \frac{6}{5}(x^2 + 2xy) dx dy = \frac{6}{5} \int_0^1 \left(\frac{y}{4} + \frac{2y^2}{3}\right) dy = \frac{6}{5}\left(\frac{25}{72}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{5}{12}}$$
> 
> **Step 3: Covariance**
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] = \frac{5}{12} - (0.7 \times 0.6) = \frac{5}{12} - \frac{21}{50} = \frac{125 - 126}{300} = \mathbf{-\frac{1}{300}}$$
> 
> **Step 4: Variances & Correlation**
> $$E[X^2] = \frac{6}{5}\left[\frac{x^5}{5} + \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = \frac{27}{50} \implies V[X] = \frac{27}{50} - (0.7)^2 = \mathbf{0.05} = \frac{1}{20}$$
> $$E[Y^2] = \frac{2}{5}\left[\frac{y^3}{3} + \frac{3y^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = \frac{13}{20} \implies V[Y] = \frac{13}{20} - (0.6)^2 = \mathbf{\frac{11}{150}} \approx 0.0733$$
> $$\rho(X, Y) = \frac{-1/300}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{20} \times \frac{11}{150}}} = \frac{-1/300}{\sqrt{\frac{11}{3000}}} \approx \mathbf{-0.055}$$
> 
> **Answer:** $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = -\frac{1}{300}$, $\rho = -0.055$ (weak negative correlation).

### Type 17: Portfolio / Sum Variance
**Problem 7.3 (Linear Combination Variance):**  
Let $X$ and $Y$ have variances $\text{Var}(X) = 25$, $\text{Var}(Y) = 16$, and correlation coefficient $\rho(X, Y) = 0.4$. Find the variance of the linear combination $W = 3X - 2Y$.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> First find $\text{Cov}(X, Y)$ using $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \rho \sigma_X \sigma_Y$:
> - $\sigma_X = \sqrt{25} = 5$
> - $\sigma_Y = \sqrt{16} = 4$
> $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0.4 \times 5 \times 4 = \mathbf{8}$$
> 
> Using the general linear variance property $\text{Var}(aX + bY) = a^2 \text{Var}(X) + b^2 \text{Var}(Y) + 2ab \, \text{Cov}(X, Y)$:
> Here $a = 3$ and $b = -2$:
> $$\text{Var}(3X - 2Y) = 3^2 \text{Var}(X) + (-2)^2 \text{Var}(Y) + 2(3)(-2) \text{Cov}(X, Y)$$
> $$\text{Var}(3X - 2Y) = 9(25) + 4(16) - 12(8)$$
> $$\text{Var}(3X - 2Y) = 225 + 64 - 96 = \mathbf{193}$$
> 
> **Answer:** $\text{Var}(3X - 2Y) = \mathbf{193}$.

## Module 9: Hypothesis Testing (Pages 1–42 & Class Notes)

### Type 19: Single Mean — Large Sample $Z$-Test
**Problem 9.1 (Classroom Lecture Problem):**  
A manufacturer claims that a machine produces items with a mean weight of $\mu = 20\text{ gm}$ and a known variance of $\sigma^2 = 300$. An auditor samples $n = 36$ items and finds a sample mean of $\bar{X} = 23\text{ gm}$. Test the manufacturer's claim at the $\alpha = 0.05$ level of significance against a two-tailed alternative.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: State Hypotheses**
> - Null hypothesis: $H_0: \mu = 20$
> - Alternative hypothesis: $H_1: \mu \neq 20$ (Two-tailed)
> 
> **Step 2: Level of Significance**
> $\alpha = 0.05$.
> 
> **Step 3: Test Statistic**
> Since $n = 36 \ge 30$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{300} \approx 17.3205$ is known:
> $$Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$$
> 
> **Step 4: Critical Values**
> For a two-tailed $Z$-test at $\alpha = 0.05$, the critical values are $\pm 1.96$.
> - Reject $H_0$ if $|Z_{\text{cal}}| > 1.96$.
> 
> **Step 5: Computation**
> Standard error: $\sigma_{\bar{X}} = \frac{\sqrt{300}}{\sqrt{36}} = \frac{17.3205}{6} \approx 2.8868$.
> $$Z_{\text{cal}} = \frac{23 - 20}{2.8868} = \frac{3}{2.8868} \approx \mathbf{1.04}$$
> 
> **Step 6: Decision & Conclusion**
> Since $|Z_{\text{cal}}| = 1.04 < 1.96$, $Z_{\text{cal}}$ falls in the **Acceptance Region**.
> We **fail to reject $H_0$**.
> **Conclusion:** There is no significant evidence at the 5% level to reject the manufacturer's claim that the mean weight is $20\text{ gm}$.

### Type 20: Single Mean — Small Sample $t$-Test
**Problem 9.2 (Book Example 16.6.7):**  
A random sample of $n = 10$ light bulbs from an assembly line has a mean life of $\bar{X} = 1540$ hours with a sample standard deviation $s = 40$ hours. Can we conclude at $\alpha = 0.05$ that the mean life of the light bulbs is greater than 1500 hours?

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Hypotheses**
> - $H_0: \mu = 1500$
> - $H_1: \mu > 1500$ (One-sided right-tailed test)
> 
> **Step 2: Significance Level & Degrees of Freedom**
> $\alpha = 0.05$, $n = 10 < 30 \implies \text{Student's } t\text{-test}$ with:
> $$\nu = n - 1 = 10 - 1 = \mathbf{9 \text{ degrees of freedom}}$$
> 
> **Step 3: Critical Value**
> For a right-tailed $t$-test with $\nu = 9$ at $\alpha = 0.05$:
> From the $t$-table, $t_{0.05, 9} = \mathbf{+1.833}$.
> 
> **Step 4: Compute Test Statistic**
> $$t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}} = \frac{1540 - 1500}{40 / \sqrt{10}} = \frac{40}{40 / 3.1623} = \frac{40}{12.649} \approx \mathbf{3.162}$$
> 
> **Step 5: Decision & Conclusion**
> Since $t_{\text{cal}} = 3.162 > 1.833$, it falls deep in the **Critical (Rejection) Region**.
> We **reject $H_0$** at the $5\%$ significance level.
> **Conclusion:** The light bulbs have a statistically significant mean life greater than 1500 hours.

### Type 21: Difference of Two Means — Large Sample $Z$-Test
**Problem 9.3 (Comparing Two Factory Outputs):**  
Factory A produces steel beams with a sample mean tensile strength of $\bar{X}_1 = 800\text{ kg}$ ($n_1 = 50, s_1 = 20$). Factory B produces beams with $\bar{X}_2 = 790\text{ kg}$ ($n_2 = 40, s_2 = 25$). Test whether the mean strength of beams from Factory A is significantly higher than Factory B at $\alpha = 0.01$.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Hypotheses**
> - $H_0: \mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0$ (Equal strength)
> - $H_1: \mu_1 - \mu_2 > 0$ (Factory A is stronger, right-tailed test)
> 
> **Step 2: Significance Level**
> $\alpha = 0.01$.
> 
> **Step 3: Test Statistic**
> Both samples are large ($n_1 = 50 \ge 30, n_2 = 40 \ge 30$), so use the two-sample $Z$-test:
> $$Z_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$$
> 
> **Step 4: Critical Value**
> For a right-tailed $Z$-test at $\alpha = 0.01$:
> $$Z_{\text{crit}} = \mathbf{+2.33}$$
> 
> **Step 5: Computation**
> Standard error:
> $$\text{SE} = \sqrt{\frac{20^2}{50} + \frac{25^2}{40}} = \sqrt{\frac{400}{50} + \frac{625}{40}} = \sqrt{8 + 15.625} = \sqrt{23.625} \approx 4.8605$$
> 
> $$Z_{\text{cal}} = \frac{800 - 790}{4.8605} = \frac{10}{4.8605} \approx \mathbf{2.057}$$
> 
> **Step 6: Decision**
> Since $Z_{\text{cal}} = 2.057 < 2.33$, it does **not** fall in the critical region.
> We **fail to reject $H_0$** at the $1\%$ level of significance.
> **Conclusion:** There is insufficient evidence at $\alpha = 0.01$ to conclude that Factory A produces stronger beams.

### Type 22: Difference of Two Means — Independent Small Samples Pooled $t$-Test
**Problem 9.4:**  
Two independent groups of students were taught statistics using two different methods. Exam scores yielded:
- Group 1 ($n_1 = 8$): $\bar{X}_1 = 76, \quad s_1 = 6$
- Group 2 ($n_2 = 10$): $\bar{X}_2 = 71, \quad s_2 = 5$

Assuming equal population variances ($\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$), test whether there is a significant difference in mean performance at $\alpha = 0.05$.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Hypotheses**
> - $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 \implies \mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0$
> - $H_1: \mu_1 \neq \mu_2$ (Two-tailed)
> 
> **Step 2: Degrees of Freedom**
> $$\nu = n_1 + n_2 - 2 = 8 + 10 - 2 = \mathbf{16 \text{ df}}$$
> 
> **Step 3: Critical Value**
> For a two-tailed test with $\nu = 16$ at $\alpha = 0.05$:
> From the $t$-table, $t_{0.025, 16} = \mathbf{\pm 2.120}$.
> 
> **Step 4: Compute Pooled Variance $s_p^2$**
> $$s_p^2 = \frac{(n_1 - 1)s_1^2 + (n_2 - 1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2} = \frac{(7)(6^2) + (9)(5^2)}{16} = \frac{(7 \times 36) + (9 \times 25)}{16}$$
> $$s_p^2 = \frac{252 + 225}{16} = \frac{477}{16} \approx \mathbf{29.8125} \implies s_p = \sqrt{29.8125} \approx 5.460$$
> 
> **Step 5: Compute Test Statistic**
> $$t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{s_p \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}}} = \frac{76 - 71}{5.460 \sqrt{\frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{10}}} = \frac{5}{5.460 \sqrt{0.125 + 0.10}}$$
> $$t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{5}{5.460 \times \sqrt{0.225}} = \frac{5}{5.460 \times 0.4743} = \frac{5}{2.5899} \approx \mathbf{1.931}$$
> 
> **Step 6: Decision**
> Since $|t_{\text{cal}}| = 1.931 < 2.120$, it falls in the **Acceptance Region**.
> We **fail to reject $H_0$** at $\alpha = 0.05$.
> **Conclusion:** The difference between the two teaching methods is not statistically significant.

### Type 23: Paired Samples $t$-Test (Book Table 16.13, p. 41)
**Problem 9.5 (Before-and-After Training):**  
Ten technicians ($n = 10$) attended an intensive circuit assembly workshop. Their error rates before and after the workshop were measured. The individual reduction in errors ($d_i = \text{Before} - \text{After}$) produced:
- Sum of reductions: $\sum d_i = 40$
- Sum of squared reductions: $\sum d_i^2 = 284$

Test at the $\alpha = 0.05$ level whether the workshop produced a significant reduction in assembly errors.

> [!success]- View Complete Solution
> **Step 1: Hypotheses**
> - $H_0: \mu_d = 0$ (No reduction in errors)
> - $H_1: \mu_d > 0$ (Positive reduction; right-tailed test)
> 
> **Step 2: Sample Mean & Variance of Differences**
> - Mean difference: $\bar{d} = \frac{\sum d_i}{n} = \frac{40}{10} = \mathbf{4.0}$
> - Sample variance:
>   $$s_d^2 = \frac{\sum d_i^2 - \frac{(\sum d_i)^2}{n}}{n - 1} = \frac{284 - \frac{40^2}{10}}{10 - 1} = \frac{284 - 160}{9} = \frac{124}{9} \approx 13.778$$
>   $$s_d = \sqrt{13.778} \approx \mathbf{3.712}$$
> 
> **Step 3: Degrees of Freedom & Critical Value**
> $$\nu = n - 1 = 10 - 1 = \mathbf{9 \text{ df}}$$
> For a right-tailed test with $\nu = 9$ at $\alpha = 0.05$:
> $$t_{\text{crit}} = t_{0.05, 9} = \mathbf{+1.833}$$
> 
> **Step 4: Compute Test Statistic**
> $$t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{d} - 0}{s_d / \sqrt{n}} = \frac{4.0}{3.712 / \sqrt{10}} = \frac{4.0}{3.712 / 3.1623} = \frac{4.0}{1.1738} \approx \mathbf{3.408}$$
> 
> **Step 5: Decision**
> Since $t_{\text{cal}} = 3.408 > 1.833$, it falls far inside the **Critical Region**.
> We **reject $H_0$** at $\alpha = 0.05$.
> **Conclusion:** The workshop produced a statistically significant reduction in assembly errors.
