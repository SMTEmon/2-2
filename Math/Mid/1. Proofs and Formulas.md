# Probability & Statistics: Formulas and Proofs Cheat Sheet

## 1. Set Theory & Basic Probability Rules

> [!note] Set Operations & Laws
> - **Union:** $A \cup B$
> - **Intersection:** $A \cap B$
> - **Complement:** $A^c$ or $\bar{A}$
> - **Mutually Exclusive:** $A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$ (for $i \neq j$)
> - **Collectively Exhaustive:** $\bigcup_{i=1}^{n} A_i = S$
> - **De Morgan’s Laws:** 
>   1. $(A \cup B)^c = A^c \cap B^c$
>   2. $(A \cap B)^c = A^c \cup B^c$

> [!note] Probability Axioms & Fundamental Rules
> - **Axiom 1:** $P(S) = 1$
> - **Axiom 2:** $0 \le P(E) \le 1$
> - **Axiom 3:** $P(E_1 \cup E_2) = P(E_1) + P(E_2)$ (if $E_1 \cap E_2 = \emptyset$)
> - **Rule of Complements:** $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)$
> - **General Addition Rule:** $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$
> - **Equally Likely Outcomes:** $P(E) = \frac{n(E)}{N}$

## 2. Advanced Probability & Bayes' Theorem

> [!note] Conditional & Joint Probability
> - **Conditional Probability:** $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$
> - **Multiplication Rule:** $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B|A) = P(B) \cdot P(A|B)$
> - **Multiplication Rule (n events):** 
>   $$P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap \dots \cap A_n) = P(A_1)P(A_2|A_1)P(A_3|A_1 \cap A_2)\dots P(A_n|A_1 \cap \dots \cap A_{n-1})$$

> [!note] Total Probability Rule
> $$P(B) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A_j \cap B) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A_j) \cdot P(B|A_j)$$

> [!theorem] Bayes' Theorem
> $$P(A_i|B) = \frac{P(A_i) \cdot P(B|A_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A_j) \cdot P(B|A_j)}$$

> [!abstract] Proof of Bayes' Theorem
> 1. From conditional probability: $P(A_i|B) = \frac{P(A_i \cap B)}{P(B)}$
> 2. Using distributive law for a partition $A_1 \dots A_n$: $B = (A_1 \cap B) \cup (A_2 \cap B) \cup \dots \cup (A_n \cap B)$
> 3. Summing mutually exclusive events: $P(B) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A_j \cap B)$
> 4. Applying multiplication rule: $P(B) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A_j) \cdot P(B|A_j)$
> 5. Applying multiplication rule to numerator: $P(A_i \cap B) = P(A_i) \cdot P(B|A_i)$
> 6. Substituting (4) and (5) into (1):
>    $$P(A_i|B) = \frac{P(A_i) \cdot P(B|A_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(A_j) \cdot P(B|A_j)} \quad \blacksquare$$

## 3. Univariate Random Variables (1D)

> [!note] Discrete Random Variables (PMF & CDF)
> - **PMF Conditions:** 
>   1. $f(x) \ge 0$
>   2. $\sum_{x} f(x) = 1$
> - **CDF:** $F(x) = P(X \le x) = \sum_{t \le x} f(t)$

> [!note] Continuous Random Variables (PDF & CDF)
> - **PDF Conditions:**
>   1. $f(x) \ge 0$
>   2. $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x) dx = 1$
> - **Probability as Area:** $P(a < X < b) = \int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx$
> - **CDF:** $F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f(t) dt$
> - **PDF from CDF:** $f(x) = \frac{d}{dx} F(x)$

---

## 4. Bivariate Random Variables (Joint, Marginal, Conditional)

> [!note] Joint Distributions
> - **Discrete Joint PMF Condition:** $\sum_x \sum_y f(x,y) = 1$
> - **Continuous Joint PDF Condition:** $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y) \, dx \, dy = 1$

> [!note] Marginal Distributions
> **Discrete:**
> - $g(x) = \sum_{y} f(x,y)$
> - $h(y) = \sum_{x} f(x,y)$
> 
> **Continuous:**
> - $g(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y) \, dy$
> - $h(y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y) \, dx$

> [!note] Conditional Distributions
> - **$Y$ given $X$:** $f(y|x) = \frac{f(x,y)}{g(x)}$
> - **$X$ given $Y$:** $f(x|y) = \frac{f(x,y)}{h(y)}$
> - **Conditional Interval (Continuous):** $P(a < X < b \mid Y = y) = \int_{a}^{b} f(x|y) \, dx$

> [!note] Independence Conditions
> Random variables $X$ and $Y$ are independent iff:
> $$f(x,y) = g(x) \cdot h(y)$$
> Or alternatively: $f(y|x) = h(y)$ and $f(x|y) = g(x)$.

## 5. Mathematical Expectation, Variance & MGF

> [!note] Expected Value (Mean), $\mu$ or $E(X)$
> - **Discrete:** $E(X) = \sum_{x} x f(x)$
> - **Continuous:** $E(X) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f(x) \, dx$
> - **Function of X:** $E[W(X)] = \sum_{x} w(x) f(x)$ OR $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} w(x) f(x) \, dx$

> [!theorem] Theorems of Expectation
> 1. $E(c) = c$
> 2. $E(aX + b) = aE(X) + b$
> 3. $E(X + Y) = E(X) + E(Y)$
> 4. $E(XY) = E(X) \cdot E(Y)$ *(Only if X and Y are independent)*

> [!abstract] Proof: $E(c) = c$
> $$E(c) = \sum_{x} c f(x)$$
> $$E(c) = c \sum_{x} f(x)$$
> Since $\sum f(x) = 1$:
> $$E(c) = c \cdot 1 = c \quad \blacksquare$$

> [!note] Variance and Standard Deviation
> - **Variance Formula:** $V(X) = \sigma^2 = E[(X - \mu)^2]$
> - **Standard Deviation:** $\sigma = \sqrt{V(X)}$
> - **Computational Formula:** $V(X) = E(X^2) - \mu^2$

> [!abstract] Proof: Computational Formula for Variance
> $$V(X) = E[(X - \mu)^2]$$
> $$V(X) = E(X^2 - 2X\mu + \mu^2)$$
> $$V(X) = E(X^2) - E(2X\mu) + E(\mu^2)$$
> Since $\mu$ is a constant:
> $$V(X) = E(X^2) - 2\mu E(X) + \mu^2$$
> Substitute $E(X) = \mu$:
> $$V(X) = E(X^2) - 2\mu(\mu) + \mu^2$$
> $$V(X) = E(X^2) - 2\mu^2 + \mu^2$$
> $$V(X) = E(X^2) - \mu^2 \quad \blacksquare$$

> [!note] Moment Generating Function (MGF)
> $$M_X(t) = E(e^{tx})$$
> - **Discrete:** $M_X(t) = \sum_{x} e^{tx} f(x)$
> - **Continuous:** $M_X(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{tx} f(x) \, dx$
