# Sidebar: Network Neutrality

Up → [[00 - Index]] · Related → [[03 - Queuing, Buffering & Scheduling]]

> [!question] What is network neutrality?
> A term with three overlapping meanings:
> - **Technical** — how an ISP should share/allocate its resources; packet scheduling and buffer management ([[03 - Queuing, Buffering & Scheduling]]) are the *mechanisms*.
> - **Social / economic** — protecting free speech, encouraging innovation and competition.
> - **Legal** — enforced rules and policies (which differ by country).

## The 2015 US FCC "bright line" rules

> [!quote] FCC Open Internet Order (2015)
> Three "clear, bright line" rules:
> 1. **No blocking** — "shall not block lawful content, applications, services, or non-harmful devices, subject to reasonable network management."
> 2. **No throttling** — "shall not impair or degrade lawful Internet traffic on the basis of Internet content, application, or service, or use of a non-harmful device, subject to reasonable network management."
> 3. **No paid prioritization** — "shall not engage in paid prioritization."

## Is an ISP a "telecommunications" or "information" service?

The regulatory classification matters a great deal in the US, under the **Telecommunications Act of 1934/1996**:

| Title | Applies to | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Title II** | "telecommunications services" | **Common carrier duties**: reasonable rates, non-discrimination, subject to regulation |
| **Title I** | "information services" | No common-carrier duties (largely unregulated), but FCC retains authority "as may be necessary in the execution of its functions" |

> [!note] Status is contested and has swung back and forth
> ISP classification under Title I vs. Title II has been reclassified multiple times by different FCC administrations since 2015, and net-neutrality rules have been adopted, repealed, and re-litigated in US courts. Different countries — the EU, India, Canada, etc. — have taken their own, sometimes stricter, positions. Because this is an actively shifting regulatory and political question, treat any specific rule as a snapshot in time rather than a permanent fact, and check current FCC/EU guidance for the present state.
